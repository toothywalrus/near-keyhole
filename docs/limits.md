# Honest limits, all of them

The README lists the ones that matter most to a buyer. This is the whole list.

- **A buyer can leak** what he read, or the keys themselves. No watermarking is attempted.
- **A dispute publishes.** Revealing the one-time secret makes that sale's nodes public, so a false dispute gives the window away. The griefer loses a deposit of at least the price to the trader, but the leak has no fix short of zero knowledge. An audit publishes one epoch's trades, which is why a trader can't be slashed without them.
- **A thin journal isn't private once its figure is posted.** An entry's block time, the number of entries in an epoch and the posted figure are public, and so are the rounds. For an epoch with one trade that is one equation with one unknown, `figure = size × (mark − fill)`, so anyone can work out its side and size with no key: one candidate per feed, and the ciphertext hides which. `npm run attack` does it ("LIMIT, shown"). When a trader writes is public as it happens. What stays sealed is the note, the claimed fill, everything in an epoch with several trades, and everything before the figure is posted, which is what a copier pays for. **Decoys narrow it, they don't close it** (`docs/decisions/0025`): a trader's agent can write entries with no quantity at other moments of the epoch, which the PnL rule voids, so the figure doesn't move and an audit still passes. With three decoys the same sum gives four candidates per feed instead of one, and nothing public says which entry was the trade ("DECOYS" in `npm run attack`). Still public: how many entries there were and when, the net figure, and the side whenever every candidate has the same sign. An audit opens every entry of its epoch in public, decoys included, so decoys hide nothing in an epoch someone has audited. A decoy costs an entry's storage deposit and gas, and nobody forces a trader to write any.
- **The first testnet run's journals keep no secret.** The previous deployment's traders (`app.near-keyhole-y4ow.testnet`, 2026-11-10) took their tree seeds from the repo's test seeds (`tools/world.ts`: a hash of a public string and the account's name), so anyone who reads this repository can open vega's and lucky's journals there. The current deployment's run (2026-11-14) made each trader's seed from 32 random bytes kept in the OS keyring (`"seeds": "keyring"` in `docs/demo/testnet-run.json`), so its journals open only with nodes someone delivered, and the Proof page drops its caveat.
- **One account can't prove it isn't one of ten.** Bond, age, trades and the deflated Sharpe price that doubt.
- **A buyer count is only as real as what it costs to fake.** A trader buying from himself pays only gas and the 5% fee.
- **Slashing yourself recovers 40% of the bond** through a second account, and no more: the rest burns, and no buyer is paid from a bond. So a proven lie always costs at least 60%, and that is the guarantee, not the whole bond.
- **A trader can refuse a window.** Delivery can't be forced, so a refusal keeps that window's figures from ever being proven false. What it can't do is keep them believed: a refused epoch's gain stops counting (a loss stays), the refusal costs at least 2% of the book per epoch on the record, the escrow goes back, and half the price burns from the bond, once per epoch withheld (`0015`).
- **An offline trader can be made to refuse.** A window that is already over can be bought at any moment, and the trader has `catchup_grace_secs` (48 h in the app, 10 minutes on testnet) to deliver. If his agent is down longer, a stranger can buy windows over past epochs and have them refused, at no cost to himself. It costs the trader what's described above, but no more than once per epoch. `set_paused` stops sales for a planned outage.
- **A late false figure leaves a buyer little time to save his escrow.** A trader may post at the last moment of the posting grace, and a window's escrow is released `challenge_secs` after the window ends. In between, a buyer who holds the keys has to run the audit: `challenge_secs − post_grace_secs` at worst, which is 60 s in the testnet configuration, 5 minutes in the one prepared for mainnet, and 8 hours in the web app's. After that the proof still slashes the bond and pays its prover 40%, but the escrow has gone to the trader and doesn't come back. `new` only requires the challenge to be longer than the grace: a deployment meant for people should set it many hours longer.
- **A window with no key registered waits for its holder.** A token passed on by a plain transfer has no one-time key until its new holder registers one, which he can do at any moment before it's settled. The trader then has `delivery_grace_secs` to deliver, paused or not, so a holder can choose that moment. A trader with such a window open should keep his agent up, or settle it as soon as its challenge ends, which anyone can do. On the first locked deployment such a window also burned half its price from the bond if the trader missed that epoch's figure; the current one burns nothing for a window nobody could be delivered (`docs/decisions/0023`).
- **A deposit sent with a request that fails stays in the contract.** `import_round` keeps its storage deposit when the oracle has nothing newer. `request_attested` forwards its deposit to OutLayer, and if that call fails the refund lands on the contract, not on the trader who asked. Neither can be claimed back. The sums are small: 0.003 and 0.03 NEAR in the testnet run's calls.
- **A publish-then-trade trader isn't caught.** Nothing forces a fill before the entry. Next-round pricing stops him profiting on the record from it, but not from trading against followers.
- **A journal is what a trader wrote.** An entry committed before the outcome is a valid record even if never executed. A settlement reference is his own claim: a random hash is accepted, and the app shows it as "claims a fill (unverified)". Real confidential trades live on mainnet, with history invite-only, and no public viewing key exists.
- **A figure with no buyers is checked by nobody** but its bond, unless the attested job has run on it. On testnet one figure has been computed in OutLayer's TEE, at the trader's request, on the previous deployment. The current deployment has the `request_attested` fix and OutLayer accepts its request, but OutLayer's worker fails the run, so no epoch carries the `OutLayerTee` label (`docs/deployments.md`).
- **On a live feed, a trader could know his fill a few seconds ahead on the first locked deployment.** A trade was priced there at the first round at or after its entry, and a Chainlink round's answer sits in Ethereum's mempool before the block that stamps it. A trader who watches it can commit an entry on NEAR in between and be filled at a price he already knew: paper PnL without risk, and an audit finds the figure true. Nothing on testnet is priced this way (every trade there uses recorded feeds). The current deployment prices at the first round at least `price_delay_secs` after the entry (`docs/decisions/0021`), with tests; the delay is a floor chosen per source chain, not a proof. It bounds a borrowed feed, whose rounds carry their source's time and can't be imported twice. On a recorded feed a round is stamped when it's recorded here, so a recorder that records the source's latest round again after the delay, or is simply slow, fills a trade at a price that was public before it: there the delay is only as good as the recorder (`0022`, and "LIMIT, shown" in `npm run attack`).
- **Recorded prices are trusted to the recorder, and on testnet they're public.** The rounds replayed on testnet are in `fixtures/chainlink-rounds.json`, so a trader there can know the next price: the demo's PnL shows the mechanism, not skill. A proven feed is only as dense as its relayers: a trader could try to relay only favourable rounds (`docs/decisions/0010`).
- **On the first locked deployment, a borrowed feed's first round could be imported however old it was,** which could price a trade already void and turn an audited figure false. Its borrowed feed already holds its first round, so it couldn't happen there; the current deployment allows an old first round only if it is older than the contract (`docs/decisions/0024`).
- **A window on a slashed trader can still change hands by a plain transfer.** This tree's source refuses to list or sell one through the contract; `nft_transfer` still moves it, and its new holder claims the refund it's worth.
- **The contract is locked, its owner account isn't.** Nobody can change the code or move the contract's NEAR. The `owner` account it was initialised with can still register local runners (their figures are labeled `SimulatedLocal`) and set which job hash OutLayer runs (`set_outlayer`), which decides what `OutLayerTee` means. Both are public views. A bug in the locked contract stays: the fix is a new deployment.
- **`OutLayerTee` trusts the owner's choice of job.** The contract gives that label only to a callback from OutLayer's own account, and pins the wasm hash, but the owner picks the hash. It's public: compare it with a rebuild. The label and the hash are taken from the configuration as it stands when the result arrives, not when it was asked for, so an owner who calls `set_outlayer` while a request is pending decides what that result is called.
- **The window is priced in epochs.** Empty windows are possible: a trader may post a flat figure and write nothing. The buyer can see the journal's public count before buying a past window, but not a future one, and the count includes decoys, so it says at most how many trades there were.
- **The fee can't change.** It's set at `new`; a different rate is a new deployment. After a window has closed, a later `dispute_entry` slashes the trader but returns no escrow, and the treasury keeps its 5% of that sale, as the trader keeps his 95%.
- **Testnet and sandbox only.** Nothing here has touched real money.
