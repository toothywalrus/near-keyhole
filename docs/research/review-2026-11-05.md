# An independent review of the contract (2026-11-05)

Once the attack suite and the fuzz passed, a separate AI reviewer with no stake in the design read the contract and looked for what they had missed. It ran nothing: each finding was traced by hand, then confirmed here. It found real bugs that 227 passing checks and 50 caught mutants hadn't. What that says about those numbers: they prove the checks are load-bearing, not that the checks are the right ones.

| # | Severity | Finding | Fix | Now tested by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical | **Any buyer could slash an honest trader.** A storage key masked its number to 32 bits, while the tag used the caller's full number: `dispute_entry` with `seq + 2^32` loaded the real entry and failed its tag | `key()` refuses a number that doesn't fit | `a number that doesn't fit is refused`, a mutant |
| 2 | High | **A resale race slashed an honest trader.** Resale stayed open during the delivery period, and a sybil's `buy_resale` landing just before an honest `deliver` turned its wrap into garbage for the new holder | A token can't move once delivery can begin, and `deliver` names the key it wrapped to (`0006`) | `RESALE RACE`, two mutants |
| 3 | High | **Self-slashing recovered the whole bond,** not the half the README claimed: buy your own window through a second account, deliver garbage, dispute. Also: third parties could buy in on the eve of a slash | A burn share and an age rule (`0012`), which the next review showed weren't enough: replaced by `0013` | `SELF-SLASH` |
| 4 | Medium | **Free reading:** a past window with a missed figure or past its floor could be bought, read and fully refunded from the bond | `buy_window` refuses a window whose refund is already owed | `KNOWN BAD` twice, two mutants |
| 5 | Medium | **A dead feed blocked audits for good:** one trade on a feed with no further rounds, then lie freely. (Found here while the review ran, and by the review independently.) | A trade with no round in `max_round_gap_secs` is void; a late round can't change a figure (`0004`) | `DEAD FEED`, three mutants |
| 6 | Low | A token returned by `nft_on_transfer` kept a listing its receiver had set | Cleared on return | `a token that comes back has no listing`, a mutant |
| 7 | Low | A figure posted just before an unbonding delay ended could leave unaudited | The bond stays at risk for `unbond_secs` after the last figure | `LATE LIE`, a mutant |

The reviewer also traced `obligations`, `bond` and `liability` through every transition and found no double-pay or drift, which agrees with the fuzz.

## What the mutants found next

After these fixes, the first full mutation run caught 58 of 62. The four misses: one mutant's source line had moved, so the tool reported it stale; and three were real gaps in the tests. Nothing audited a trade priced by a late round on the contract itself. No check made a receiver list a token during `nft_on_transfer`. And the unbonding delay was masked by the new last-figure rule, since every test trader had posted recently. Each gap got a check, and a single full run then caught `62/62` (`docs/research/mutation-run.txt`).

## A second read: an AI judge's dry run (2026-11-05)

A reader with no build context scored the project 66/100. Everything reproduced and was honestly labeled, and it found no way to slash an honest trader. Its findings, each reproduced here first:

| # | Finding | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bond escape:** a sybil is refused delivery and collects a penalty from the bond (10 NEAR down to 0.82 in 72 h, status `Active`) | **Fixed.** Penalties burn, and a trader below `min_bond` can't sell. Reproducing it turned up a worse variant, a deliberate lapse that moved the whole bond in one cycle: also fixed, by paying refunds from escrow (`0013`). Tests: `BOND ESCAPE`. Mutants: 4 |
| 2 | **Slash dilution:** a sybil's young window cut a real buyer's bond share from 0.600 to 0.128, and it took 58% of the bond, not 40% | **Fixed differently from what was proposed:** not by releasing young windows, but by removing the buyers' pool. Buyers get their escrow back whole, so there is nothing to dilute, and a self-slash recovers 40% at most. Test: `SELF-SLASH` |
| 3 | **OutLayer's command fails as shipped:** no `rpc` in the input, and the test injected it | **Fixed.** The contract sends it, and `tools/job.ts` runs the job on exactly that input and returns its real stdout. OutLayer's docs say wasip2 jobs can make outbound HTTP calls, with no allowlist mentioned. The hosted run hasn't been tried |
| 4 | **Losers can be skipped:** winners only gave true −$400, public +$200 | **Fixed.** The `score` view charges each gap, and the board shows gaps. The reader's "−floor" became the worse of the floor and 2% of the book, since a trader chooses his floor. Test: `SKIPPED LOSER`. Mutants: 2 |
| 5 | **"Checkable" counted a paid window with no keys** | **Fixed.** Counted on delivery, and reduced on proven garbage. Test: `CHECKABLE`. Mutant: 1 |
| 6 | **A junk entry blocks an audit for good,** and after settlement the false figure stood | **Fixed.** `audit_cancel`, and `dispute_entry` works after settlement. Test: `JUNK ENTRY`. Mutants: 3 |
| 7 | **"Settled" is self-asserted, and "fill comes first" is unenforced** | **Conceded, and reworded** (`0014`). It can't be verified in the contract without a public view of real trades |
| 8 | **"Byte for byte" only at the author's path,** and `build.sh` overwrote the hashes it was checked against | **Fixed.** Paths are remapped, and `npm run check-res` builds at another path and compares against the committed hashes |
| 9 | **A mock earned the `OutLayerTee` label; the contract keeps a FullAccess key** | **First fixed:** a stand-in now gets its own label (a mutant). **Second disclosed, not locked:** locking would strand the demo's funds, and it's a testnet demo. `npm run verify` prints the key count |

From the same reader:

- `npm run gas` waited undocumented and dirtied tracked files: **fixed**, it writes only with `--write`, and the wait is documented. So does `npm run demo`.
- A trader can refuse delivery of past windows to keep old false figures unauditable: **true, and partly addressed.** It costs half the price and a charge on the public figure per epoch, and shows on the board. Delivery can't be forced. **Stated as a limit.**
- On testnet the replayed rounds are public, so a trader knows future prices: **true, and now stated.** The recorder path is for showing the mechanism.
- P(real) read backwards, 86% for 9 trades against 27% for 84: **fixed.** The formula's denominator falls toward zero on a lumpy, right-skewed series. It's floored now, blank below 20 days or 12 active days, and moved to a dimmer column.
- A +$11.5k figure on a 25 NEAR bond with no comparison: **fixed,** the bond shows in dollars and as a share of the PnL. The dollar value uses a fixed recorded $4.01, labeled.
- The default copy limits refused most quotes: **fixed.** The limits are your book and your cap, and the page explains what scaling does and why a big trade is refused.
- Phone ruler labels illegible: **fixed,** hidden at phone width, where the caption carries them.
- **The key covers a self-written journal, and copying was CLI and sandbox only.** Partly narrowed: "Copy it here" signs a real intent in the tab and settles it on the real `intents.near` wasm, with a local solver. The journal limit stands (`0014`): no public viewing key exists, so this is a signal record, not a brokerage statement.

**Disputed:** one fix proposed for finding 2 (permissionless release of young windows, penalty through `bond_share`): removing the pool closes more, and more simply. And "−floor" in finding 4, for the reason above. No finding was disputed on its substance.

The first mutation pass after these fixes caught 68 of 73. All five misses were real gaps in the checks: a refusal that was checked only if the loop happened to reach it, a cancel tested after the audit was gone, a label tested only outside the suite, and two refusals never tried on `dispute_entry`. Each got a check, and `docs/research/mutation-run.txt` is the single full pass after that.

**Still open, created by the fix for finding 1:** a breached window now burns half its price from the bond while the buyer gets the escrow back. So a stranger can buy windows on an honest trader in a drawdown, at no cost, and burn his bond up to the coverage limit. Nobody profits, which is strictly better than before, when the stranger was paid from the bond, but the trader still loses. Filed as #15; it needs a split penalty and a redeploy.
