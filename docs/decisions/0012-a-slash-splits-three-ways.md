# 0012: A slash splits three ways, and a window bought on its eve shares nothing

**Context.** The first version split a slashed bond two ways: half to the prover, the rest pro rata to open windows. An independent review found that a trader could recover all of it. Through a second account he buys a big window, delivers garbage, disputes, and takes the prover's half plus the whole pro-rata share. It takes three transactions, so a liar could pre-empt an open audit, and the auditor would get nothing. Anyone watching a multi-transaction audit could also buy in just before its end, for a risk-free share. And an honest buyer's share got diluted by both.

**Decision.** `prover_bps` (40%) goes to the prover. `burn_bps` (20%) burns: it stops being owed, is claimable by nobody, and stays in the contract. The rest backs refunds. After a slash, a window bought within `challenge_secs` before it gets its escrow back and nothing from the bond (`bond_share`).

**Consequences.** A trader slashing himself through a fresh account recovers 40% at most, and the rest is gone. With an old window he can take a pro-rata share too, but that needs his own money sat in escrow for the challenge period, in competition with real buyers. It isn't zero: stated in the README. An auditor can still be pre-empted by a self-slash, but the trader loses the majority of the bond and is marked for good either way, which is what the audit was for. Claim order can shift shares slightly, since a young window's price leaves the liability without drawing from the bond; claims can never exceed the bond.
